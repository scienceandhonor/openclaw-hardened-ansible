## October 19 — GOOD HABITS CROWD OUT BAD ONES

> *"Since habits have such a powerful influence on us, and we follow our impulses to achieve or avoid what is outside our control, we should always — when external impressions are hard to grasp — fall back on the power of our trained patterns of behavior."*
>
> — Epictetus, *Discourses*, 3.12.6

When a dog barks because someone is at the door, the worst thing you can do is shout. To the dog, it sounds like you're barking too! When a dog runs away, chasing after it is useless — in this case too, it's similar: now you're both running. A better choice in both scenarios is to distract the dog. Tell it to sit. Tell it to go to its bed or its kennel. Run in the other direction. Change the pattern, interrupt the negative impulse.

The same applies to us. When a bad habit surfaces, consciously counter it with the opposite behavior. Take for example that you're putting everything off today — don't indulge in it, fight back against it. Go for a walk to clear your head and recalibrate. When you catch yourself saying something negative or ugly, don't be hard on yourself. Add something positive and kind to put the remark in perspective.

Resist your ingrained habits and use the power of what you have learned to gain momentum and develop further. When you notice that in sports or on a project you're looking for shortcuts, say to yourself: "Alright then, now I'll run even further — or do it even better."

Good habits crowd out bad ones. And we know only too well how quickly we pick something up.

## August 30 — WHEN YOU ARE SLUGGISH

> *"Everything that must be done, virtue can handle bravely and swiftly. For anyone would call it foolishness if someone began a task lazily and without enthusiasm, or moved their body in one direction while their mind went in the other — letting themselves be torn apart by very different impulses."*
>
> — Seneca, *Moral Letters*, 31.b–32

When you start something and immediately notice how unenthusiastic you are and begin to feel irritated, ask yourself: *Why am I doing this?* If it's a genuinely necessary task: *Why am I being so resistant? Am I afraid? Am I against it? Am I tired?*

There is no point in pushing on while hoping that someone will come along and take this task — the one you don't want to do — off your hands. Or that someone else will suddenly explain to you why what you are doing matters. Don't be the kind of person who says yes but whose actions convey a no. In 2005, when Apple was only halfway through its impressive rise to becoming one of the most successful companies in the world, Steve Jobs told *Business Week*: "Quality is much more important than quantity... A home run is much better than two double plays."

## June 11 — DON'T MAKE IT WORSE

> *"How much more harmful are the consequences of anger and grief than the circumstances that provoked them in us!"*
>
> — Marcus Aurelius, *Meditations*, 11.18.8

There is a saying: "The first rule when you find yourself in a hole is: stop digging." This is probably the most misunderstood piece of common wisdom there is. Because when something happens, goes wrong, or we're confronted with the unexpected, we do exactly that — we only make it worse. First, by reacting with anger or grief, and then by lashing out blindly without having a plan.

Set yourself the easiest possible task for today: simply don't make things worse. Whatever happens, don't throw anger and negative feelings onto the scales. Don't react merely because you think you have to react. Let things be as they are. Stop digging. Plan your way out.
